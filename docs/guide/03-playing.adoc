IMPORTANT: Networked play is not available yet (<<01-introduction.adoc#status,Status>>). This chapter
describes the game as JXPilot implements it from XPilot 4.5.5: the key bindings and the HUD are
already in the code, and the `--demo` viewer shows what the screen will look like.

[[basics]]
== The basics

You fly a ship that turns, thrusts and fires. There are no brakes: to stop, turn around and thrust the
other way. Gravity (global, or from gravity blocks on the map) pulls on you, and hitting a wall too
hard kills you. Thrusting, shields and many items use fuel; refuel by holding the refuel key near a
fuel station. When you die, you restart at your home base.

Your shield protects you from shots and collisions while it is up, but it uses fuel and you cannot
fire while shielded.

[[controls]]
== Controls

JXPilot uses XPilot's default keys (from the C client's `default.c`). The most important ones:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Key |Function
|kbd:[A] / kbd:[S] |Turn left / right
|kbd:[Shift] (left or right) |Thrust
|kbd:[Enter] |Fire
|kbd:[Space] |Shield
|kbd:[F] or kbd:[Ctrl] |Refuel (at a fuel station); kbd:[F] also repairs (at a target)
|kbd:[Tab] |Drop a mine
|kbd:[ \ ] |Fire a smart missile
|kbd:[;] |Fire a heat seeker
|kbd:['] |Fire a torpedo
|kbd:[/] |Fire the laser
|kbd:[Page Down] / kbd:[Page Up] / kbd:[Up] |Lock on the next / previous / closest player
|kbd:[H] or kbd:[Home] |Change home base (and toggle the autopilot, kbd:[H])
|kbd:[Pause] |Pause, only on your home base (<<pausing>>)
|kbd:[End] |Self-destruct after a 150-frame countdown; press again to cancel
|kbd:[M] |Talk (type a message, kbd:[Enter] sends it)
|kbd:[Esc] |Swap between your two engine settings (<<engine-settings>>)
|Keypad kbd:[*] / kbd:[/] |More / less engine power
|Keypad kbd:[+] / kbd:[-] |Faster / slower turning
|===

[[laptop-keys]]
TIP: *Mac laptops and other compact keyboards* have no Page Up, Page Down, End or keypad keys of their
own. On a Mac laptop, kbd:[fn+↑] and kbd:[fn+↓] are Page Up and Page Down (lock on the previous / next
player), and kbd:[fn+→] is End (self-destruct). The single kbd:[Control] key works as the left Control
key: refuel, and the ball connector.

[[engine-settings]]
=== Engine settings

Your ship's engine power, turn speed and turn resistance are settings you choose. JXPilot starts you
with the XPilot client's defaults: power 45, turn speed 35 and turn resistance 0.12. kbd:[Esc] swaps to
the alternative settings, power 35, turn speed 25, turn resistance 0.12, and back. The keypad keys
above change the current settings.

[[hud]]
== The screen and the HUD

image::demo-hud.png[The --demo viewer: radar, scoreboard, HUD and messages,align=center]

The picture shows the `--demo` viewer, which draws fake data with the real painters. In a game you see:

Radar (top left):: The whole map in miniature: walls, your own ship (a heading line), other ships
when `playersOnRadar` is on, and other objects per the `...OnRadar` server options. On race maps it
also marks the next checkpoint.
Scoreboard (left, under the radar):: Players with team, score and name. Your own line is marked.
HUD (around your ship):: Dashed HUD lines, your fuel gauge and fuel meter, the items you carry with
their counts, the player you have locked on (name and distance), time left in the round, weapon
modifiers and an "Autopilot" note when it is on.
Messages (bottom left):: Game events ("pilot04 crashed into a wall.") and chat, which fade after a
while. Crash messages say what you hit: a wall, a target, a treasure, a cannon, the edge of the world
or another ship.
Score popups (on the map):: When your score changes, the points appear on the map where you made or
lost them, with a label such as the victim's name, `[Wall]`, `[Shove]` or `[Deadliest]`.
Shots:: Shots that cannot hurt you (your team mates' shots when team immunity is on, shots of a
paused player, your own shots with `selfImmunity`) are drawn in a different colour.
Talk line (middle):: Opens with kbd:[M] for typing a chat message.

[[scoring]]
== Scoring

Scoring follows XPilot's rules (the server's `Scoring` is a port of XPilot's `score.c`):

* Killing a player moves points from the victim to you. Between equal scores this is 10 points; you
  get more for killing a better player and fewer for killing a weaker one.
* Each way of killing has a multiplier: `shotKillScoreMult`, `laserKillScoreMult`,
  `runoverKillScoreMult`, and so on. Crashing into a wall costs points (`crashScoreMult`), as does
  killing yourself.
* In team play, scores can be shared within a team (`teamShareScore`); killing a team mate earns
  nothing. When a player leaves, their share is taken back and shared among the rest of the team.
* When a player leaves the game, their shots in flight disappear, unless the server sets `keepShots`.
* A paused player slowly loses points (0.01 per frame).

[[rounds]]
== Rounds, lives and game over

Without limits, the game never ends: you die, restart at your base and play on. The server options
below turn it into rounds. They are ported from XPilot and run in the server simulation today.

Limited lives:: With `limitedLives` on, each player has `worldLives` lives per round. Each death costs
one; after the last you are out of the round (marked `D` in the scoreboard) until it ends.
End of a round:: Without teams, the round ends when only one of several players is still alive (the
winner), when nobody is (a draw), or when the round time `maxRoundTime` (seconds) runs out (a draw).
In team play, a team is out when all its members are; the last team alive wins "by staying alive", and
when the time runs out the team that saved more treasures, then the team with the higher total score,
wins.
Bonuses:: At the end of a round the player with the best kills/(deaths+1) ratio gets a bonus as the
"Deadliest Player" (several tied players share it), and the winner, or the winning team's members, get
a winner's bonus.
Next round:: Everyone gets their lives back. With `reset` on (the default), every ship restarts at its base and all
shots and objects are cleared. The next round starts after `roundDelay` seconds ("Delaying N seconds
until start of next round."). With `roundsToPlay` set, the server announces "Round N out of M" and
after the last round declares "Game over..." with the best and worst players (and teams).
Joining during a round:: With limited lives, if a player (outside team play) has already lost a life
in this round, a newcomer waits for the next round, marked `W` in the scoreboard. With `resetOnHuman`
set to N, while at most N human players are in the game a newcomer ends the current round so that they
can play at once.

All of these options are in <<05-server-options.adoc#opts-race,Races, rounds and game length>> and
<<05-server-options.adoc#opts-ships,Ships and players>>.

NOTE: Race mode (checkpoints and laps) and treasure rules do not end rounds yet; they come with
milestone M2. On race maps the game currently runs without rounds.

[[pausing]]
== Pausing

* kbd:[Pause] pauses you only when you are on your own home base and nearly stopped (slower than
  3 plus the local gravity). Anywhere else the key does nothing.
* A paused ship is taken out of play and marked `P` in the scoreboard. You lose 0.01 points per frame
  while paused.
* You can unpause only after a 10-second countdown. Press kbd:[Pause] again; you restart at your home
  base. With limited lives, outside team play, if you had already lost a life this round you wait for
  the next round instead (`W`).
* If you press no keys for 60 seconds while another human is playing, you are paused automatically.
* A player paused for longer than `maxPauseTime` seconds (default 3600; 0 disables it) is removed from
  the server.

All multipliers are in <<05-server-options.adoc#opts-scoring,Scoring options>>.

[[items]]
== Items

NOTE: Items are server rules. When the JXPilot client plays on an original C XPilot server, every
item works: the C server handles pickup and use, and the client draws the items and sends their keys.
The JXPilot (Java) server does not have items yet; they come with milestone M2. The "Works on" column
shows both.

Items float around the map (more around item concentrators, the `%` map symbol); fly over one to pick
it up. On the map an item is a blue triangle with the item's symbol inside it, as in the "In play"
column; the HUD shows the symbols of the items you carry. These are XPilot's 21 items. The pictures
are drawn from the same bitmaps the client uses (ported from the XPilot client), and the descriptions
are the XPilot client's own.
