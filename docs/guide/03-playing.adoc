This chapter describes the game as JXPilot implements it from XPilot 4.5.5. To start a game, see
<<02-quick-start.adoc#run-client,Playing right away>>.

[[basics]]
== The basics

You fly a ship that turns, thrusts and fires. There are no brakes: to stop, turn around and thrust the
other way. Gravity (global, or from gravity blocks on the map) pulls on you, and hitting a wall too
hard kills you. Thrusting, shields and many items use fuel; refuel by holding the refuel key near a
fuel station. When you die, you restart at your home base.

Your shield protects you from shots and collisions while it is up, but it uses fuel and you cannot
fire while shielded. As in the XPilot client ("autoShield"), your shield is up when you start; firing
lowers it while you fire and raises it again when you stop. Once you have pressed and released the
shield key, it works as hold-to-shield.

[[controls]]
== Controls

JXPilot uses XPilot's default keys (from the C client's `default.c`), with a few additions. Press
kbd:[F1] in the game for a list of every key; the message `F1: keys` reminds you when you join. The most
important keys:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|Key |Function
|kbd:[A] / kbd:[S] |Turn left / right
|kbd:[Shift] (left or right) |Thrust
|kbd:[Enter] |Fire
|kbd:[Space] |Shield
|kbd:[F] or kbd:[Ctrl] |Refuel (at a fuel station); kbd:[F] also repairs (at a target)
|kbd:[Ctrl] or kbd:[Alt]/kbd:[Option] |Connect to a ball
|kbd:[Tab] |Drop a mine
|kbd:[ \ ] |Fire a smart missile
|kbd:[;] |Fire a heat seeker
|kbd:['] |Fire a torpedo
|kbd:[/] |Fire the laser
|kbd:[Page Down] / kbd:[Page Up] / kbd:[Up] |Lock on the next / previous / closest player
|kbd:[H] or kbd:[Home] |Change home base (and toggle the autopilot, kbd:[H])
|kbd:[Pause] |Pause, only on your home base (<<pausing>>)
|kbd:[End] or kbd:[0] |Self-destruct after a 150-frame countdown; press again to cancel
|Keypad kbd:[*] / kbd:[/] |More / less engine power (<<engine-settings>>)
|Keypad kbd:[+] / kbd:[-] |Faster / slower turning
|kbd:[M] |Talk (type a message, kbd:[Enter] sends it; <<talking>>)
|kbd:[F1] |Show or hide the key help
|kbd:[-] |Sound off and on (<<sound>>)
|kbd:[Esc] |Swap to the alternative engine settings (<<engine-settings>>), as in XPilot
|===

To quit, close the window, press kbd:[Cmd+Q] on macOS, or click QUIT in the button bar above the score
list (<<button-bar>>), as in XPilot. `keyQuit` (JXPilot only) binds a quit key; it is unbound by
default.
For kbd:[Esc] to quit, as in earlier JXPilot versions, put these two lines in `~/.xpilotrc`
(<<key-bindings>>):

[source,text]
----
xpilot.keySwapSettings:
xpilot.keyQuit:          Escape
----

JXPilot's additions to XPilot's defaults: kbd:[Alt]/kbd:[Option] also connects to a ball, so a Mac
laptop's single kbd:[Control] can stay refuel; kbd:[0] also self-destructs, since kbd:[End] needs kbd:[fn]
on a Mac laptop; kbd:[F1] shows the key help (XPilot uses it for talk macro 1); and kbd:[-] mutes. All of
this can be changed (<<key-bindings>>).

[[laptop-keys]]
TIP: *Mac laptops and other compact keyboards* have no Page Up, Page Down, End or keypad keys of their
own. On a Mac laptop, kbd:[fn+↑] and kbd:[fn+↓] are Page Up and Page Down (lock on the previous / next
player), and kbd:[fn+→] is End. The power and turn speed keys are on the keypad; the
<<mac-laptop-keymap,Mac laptop keymap>> example moves them to function keys.

[[button-bar]]
=== The button bar, CONFIG and ABOUT

Under the radar, as in XPilot, are three buttons. A button acts when you release the mouse button over
it.

QUIT:: Leave the game.
ABOUT:: XPilot's information window, four pages: the bonus items (two pages, with their pictures),
the game objective and about XPilot. NEXT, PREV and CLOSE page through it.
MENU:: Hold the mouse button and release over an entry: KEYS shows the key help (as kbd:[F1]),
CONFIG opens the settings panel, SCORE and PLAYER switch the score list between player names and ID
mode (`NICK=USER@HOST`), and MOTD shows the server's message of the day again.

*CONFIG* covers the score list with XPilot's settings, in XPilot's order, a page at a time (NEXT, PREV,
CLOSE). A Yes/No setting toggles when clicked; a number changes with its left and right arrows. Changes
apply at once: the engine settings are sent to the server, and the HUD, message, item and zoom settings
change what you see. The last entry, *Save Configuration*, writes the settings to `~/.xpilotrc` (or
the `--keys` file) in XPilot's format: each saved setting's line is replaced where it is, your other
lines stay, new settings go at the end, and the old file is kept with `.bak` added to its name. XPilot's own
save keeps only the `xpilot.` lines of the old file.

[[engine-settings]]
=== Engine settings

Your ship's engine power, turn speed and turn resistance are settings you choose. JXPilot starts you
with power 45, turn speed 40 and turn resistance 0.12 (XPilot's client defaults, except turn speed,
which is 35 in XPilot), and the alternative settings power 35, turn speed 25, turn resistance 0.12.

The power and turn speed keys (keypad kbd:[*] kbd:[/] kbd:[+] kbd:[-]) change your settings in the client,
by 10% and 5% per press, and send them to the server, as the XPilot client does. While you change them,
power and turn speed meters show below the fuel gauge (top right) for 100 frames. XPilot swaps to the
alternative settings with kbd:[Esc] (`keySwapSettings`), and the meters show then too. The six settings can be
set in your keys file (<<key-bindings>>).

Some of these keys need a word more:

* kbd:[F2] to kbd:[F12] send your talk macros 2 to 12 (<<talk-macros>>). `keySendMsg1` and
  `keySendMsg13` to `keySendMsg20` have no key by default (kbd:[F1] is the key help).

[[key-bindings]]
=== Changing the keys: `~/.xpilotrc`

JXPilot reads key bindings and control settings from `~/.xpilotrc`, in the resource format of the
XPilot client, so an existing XPilot file works. `--keys FILE` reads another file instead.

[source,text]
----
! Comments start with '!'.
xpilot.keyFireShot:       Return KP_Enter    ! the function's keys, separated by spaces
xpilot.keyPointerControl:                    ! an empty value unbinds (frees KP_Enter)
xpilot.keySwapSettings:   Insert             ! give the swap-settings function a key
xpilot.turnSpeed:         35                 ! XPilot's turn speed
----

The rules:

* One `resource: value` per line. The `xpilot.` prefix is optional (`xpilot*`, `XPilot.` and `*` work
  too); resources of other programs are ignored. A line ending in `\` continues on the next line.
* Each key function the file names *replaces* that function's default keys; functions the file does not
  name keep their defaults. An empty value unbinds the function.
* The resource names are those of the table above. Key names are X11 keysyms: letters, digits,
  `Return`, `space`, `Shift_L`, `Control_R`, `Alt_L`, `KP_Add`, `F5`, `bracketleft` and so on.
* The six control settings are `power` (5 to 55), `turnSpeed` (4 to 64), `turnResistance` (0 to 1),
  `altPower`, `altTurnSpeed` and `altTurnResistance`. Values outside the range are clamped.
* `modifierBank1` to `modifierBank4` fill the weapon modifier banks (<<modifiers>>), and `shipShape`
  and `shipShapeFile` choose your ship (<<ship-shapes>>).
* `maxVolume` (0 to 255) sets the sound volume (<<sound>>). XPilot's other sound resources, `sounds`
  and `audioServer`, are not used: the sounds are built in.
* The Command (Super) keys are never bound (kbd:[Cmd+Q] quits on macOS).
* Every problem (an unknown function or key name, a bad number, a key bound to more than one function)
  is printed when the client starts, as `jxpilot keys: FILE:LINE: ...`, and the rest of the file still
  applies.

`java -jar jxpilot-client-VERSION.jar --list-keys` (with `--keys FILE` if you use one) prints the
effective bindings, the control settings and any problems, and exits.

[[talking]]
=== Talking

kbd:[M] opens the talk line at the bottom of the screen. Type, then kbd:[Enter] sends and kbd:[Esc]
cancels. kbd:[Left] and kbd:[Right] move the cursor, kbd:[Up] and kbd:[Down] browse the lines you sent
before, kbd:[Backspace] and kbd:[Delete] erase, kbd:[Home] and kbd:[End] jump to the start and end.
As in XPilot, the server cuts a message to 79 characters, and the cursor is a blinking `_`.

*Cut and paste* (as XPilot's `selectionAndHistory`, on by default): drag with the left mouse button over
the talk line or the messages to select text; it is copied to the clipboard when you let go. The middle
mouse button pastes at the click, and so do kbd:[Ctrl+V] and, on macOS, kbd:[Cmd+V] (JXPilot only).

As in XPilot, a prefix before a colon decides who reads a line (the Java server; an original XPilot
server works the same way):

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
|You type |Who gets it
|`hello all` |Everyone. Lines show as `hello all [YourName]`.
|`2: regroup at base` |Everyone on team 2 (and you).
|`ace: nice shot` |The player whose name is `ace`, or starts with `ace` if only one does (and you).
|`god: the map is broken` |The server operator, through the server's admin message file (on a JXPilot
server, only if the operator set one up; <<04-running-a-server.adoc#admin-messages,Messages to the
operator>>). Nobody else sees it.
|`see you :-)` |Everyone: a colon followed by a smiley character, or at the end of the line, is not
an address.
|===

If nobody matches, the server tells you: "Message not sent, ace does not match any player!".

[[talk-macros]]
==== Talk macros

As in XPilot, the resources `msg1` to `msg20` in `~/.xpilotrc` hold prepared messages, and
`keySendMsg1` to `keySendMsg20` send them (kbd:[F2] to kbd:[F12] send 2 to 12 by default). A macro is
sent as if typed, so the prefixes above work. `#` starts an escape, as in XPilot:

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
|Escape |Becomes
|`##` |A `#`.
|`#t` |Your team digit (team play).
|`#h` |Your home directory.
|`#s` `#l` followed by `n`, `s`, `t` or `l` |The name, score, team or lives of yourself (`#s`) or of the
player you have locked on (`#l`; while you watch another player, that player); without one the macro
is not sent.
|`#n` |Sends what came before as a line of its own.
|`#r[a\|b\|c]` |One of the fields, at random.
|`#=[x\|y\|then\|else]` |`then` if x and y expand to the same text, else `else`.
|`#f[FILE]` |The contents of a file.
|===

A line that grows to 78 characters is sent and a new one started.

[source,text]
----
xpilot.msg2:   #ln: watch out, you are at #ls points
xpilot.msg3:   gg #r[all|everyone|folks]
----

[[pointer-control]]
=== Pointer control: steering with the mouse

With pointer control on (kbd:[Keypad Enter], or `pointerControl: yes` to start with it), moving the
mouse left and right turns your ship, on JXPilot and XPilot servers. The window takes the pointer and
hides it. Opening the talk line or leaving the window suspends pointer control until you come back.

The mouse buttons press key functions only under pointer control: `pointerButton1` to `pointerButton3`
default to `keyFireShot`, `keyThrust` and `keyShield`, and `pointerButton4` and `pointerButton5` are
the wheel, unbound by default. Each takes up to three function names (`xpilot.pointerButton2:
keyThrust keyShield`). Without pointer control, clicks go to the button bar and CONFIG.

[[sound]]
== Sound

The client plays sound effects, on by default. Which sounds play, and when, is decided as in XPilot:
the server sends XPilot's sound events (shots, explosions, hits, pickups, wormholes, wins and so on),
already made quieter with distance, and the client plays a clip for each. You also hear the start sound
when you join, as in XPilot.

On an original XPilot server you usually hear nothing: sound is compiled out of the standard XPilot
server builds, so most XPilot servers send no sound events. The client asks for sounds once, when it
joins (unless sound is off then); muting later does not change that request.

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Control |Effect
|`--no-sound` (or `--sound`) |Turn sound off (on) for this run. JXPilot only: whether an XPilot
client has sound is decided when it is built.
|`--volume N` |XPilot's `maxVolume`: the loudest a sound may play, 0 to 255. `0` means no sound; values
above 100 play like 100. Without the flag, `maxVolume` from your keys file (`xpilot.maxVolume: 60` in
`~/.xpilotrc` or the `--keys` file) applies, else 100.
|kbd:[-] |Mute and unmute while playing (`keyToggleSound`); the message log shows `Sound off.` / `Sound on.`
|===

Details:

* XPilot's 82 sound events map to 22 clips (`sounds/sounds.map` in the client jar): for example every
  pickup shares one clip and every large explosion another. Refuelling is silent, as no XPilot server
  plays its sound.
* Two things differ from XPilot: the engine sound is a loop that keeps playing while you thrust (XPilot
  replays a short sound every frame), and XPilot has no mute key.
* If there is no audio device, or the audio library cannot load, the client prints
  `no sound: REASON` and plays on silently.
* The sound effects are public domain (CC0), by Juhani Junkala and Kenney
  (<<09-appendices.adoc#third-party,Third-party components>>).

[[hud]]
== The screen and the HUD

The picture shows the `--demo` viewer, which draws fake data with the real painters. In a game you see:

Radar (top left):: The whole map in miniature: walls, your own ship (a heading line), other ships

Scoreboard (left, under the radar):: Players with team, score and name. Your own line is marked.

[[long-distance]]
== Playing over long distances

On a JXPilot server, the client hides most of the delay between you and the server. Your ship turns,
thrusts and fires on the next screen frame, at any distance: about 4 ms after the key on average, 8 ms
at most, measured at round trip times (RTT) up to 300 ms with jitter and packet loss. Without this, every
key would take a full round trip to the server and back before you saw it.

The client does this by *predicting*: it runs the same ship physics as the server (thrust, turning,
gravity, walls and bounces), from the server's exact state of your ship, with your keys, ahead of the
server's frames. When the server's verdict arrives, the client checks it against its prediction. They
usually agree to the bit.

Prediction needs a JXPilot server. On an original XPilot server (`xp://`) nothing is predicted, and the
game plays as before (<<07-client-reference.adoc#xpilot-protocol,Playing on original XPilot servers>>).

[[predicted]]
=== What is predicted

* *Your ship*: turning, thrust, gravity, walls, bounces and recoil, and the camera that follows it.
* *Your exhaust and your shield as drawn on your ship*, from your keys.
* *Your shots*: drawn the moment you fire, then checked against the server's. A shot the server did not
  fire (out of fuel, the shot limit reached) disappears.
* *Everybody else's shots, and the cannons'*: drawn where they will be when a key you press now reaches
  the server, moved with the server's own rules for shots (gravity, walls). What you dodge on screen is
  what you dodge on the server.

[[not-predicted]]
=== What is not predicted

* *Other ships* are drawn slightly in the past: about half your RTT plus 90 ms (the server's frame
  interval and a margin for jitter). So is everything else that is not a shot: missiles, mines, lasers,
  balls, items, asteroids and explosions. Aim a little ahead of a moving ship, more the farther away the
  server is.
* *Hits and shields are the server's call.* The shield on your ship shows at once, but the shield that
  protects you goes up when your key reaches the server: about half your RTT plus 70 ms after the press.
  Raise it early. Likewise, a shot that kills you shows after the round trip: your ship flies on for a
  moment, then explodes where the server says it died.
* Your ship is drawn from the server's frames, unpredicted, while you are dead or paused, during the
  delay before a round, in a wormhole or a hyperjump, and under the autopilot.

[[corrections]]
=== Corrections and snaps

Sometimes the server's ship differs from the predicted one: something the client could not know pushed
it. Shots, ships, sparks and tractor beams of other players; a cannon; a ball on its connector; items and
fuel picked up (they change the ship's mass); devices that use fuel; and, rarely, a wall bounce where two
choices were equally good. On a lossy link, a key can also reach the server a frame late.

The client then moves your ship to the server's path and replays your keys from there. Small corrections
glide in over a tenth of a second (faster near walls), so you see a slight drift rather than a jump. Your
ship jumps (snaps) only when it respawns, goes through a wormhole or hyperjumps, or when the correction is
larger than 10 blocks. Corrections are rare when nobody touches you, and more frequent in a close fight.

If corner bounces correct you often, the server operator can turn on `predictableBounces`
(<<04-running-a-server.adoc#predictable-bounces,Predictable bounces>>).

[[prediction-switch]]
=== Switching prediction off

Prediction is on by default. Turn it off for a game with `--no-predict`
(<<07-client-reference.adoc#game-flags,Game flags>>), or switch it while you play with keypad kbd:[3]
(`keyTogglePrediction`; kbd:[F7] in the <<mac-laptop-keymap,Mac laptop keymap>>). The switch shows nothing
on screen, so it works for blind comparisons: let a friend press it now and then, and say which feels
better.

With prediction off, your ship is drawn from the newest frame the server sent, so it reacts after the
round trip plus about 70 ms, and shots are drawn on the delayed timeline of the other ships.

[[net-stats]]
=== The net diagnostics line

kbd:[Insert] (`keyToggleNetStats`; kbd:[=] in the <<mac-laptop-keymap,Mac laptop keymap>>) shows or hides
a line at the top right of the game view:

----
rtt 182 ms  interp 91 ms  jitter 3.1 ms  loss 0.0% late 0.0%  lead 1.6  slack 0.31  corr 0.4/s 12 px
----

A figure the client does not know yet shows as `-`. One tick is one server frame, 1/14 s at the default
14 frames per second.

[cols="1,5",options="header"]
|===
|Field |Meaning
|`rtt` |Round trip time to the server, smoothed, from a ping every second.
|`interp` |How far in the past other ships are drawn, after the network delay: one server frame plus a margin.
|`jitter` |How unevenly the server's frames arrive.
|`loss` |Server frames lost on the way, over the last 2 seconds.
|`late` |Server frames that arrived out of order, after a newer one.
|`lead` |How many ticks ahead of the server your keys are sent: about half your RTT plus `slack`.
|`slack` |The server's report: the smallest margin, in ticks, by which your keys arrived before they were
needed, over the last second. The client keeps it a little above zero; below zero, keys arrived too late
and the client sends them earlier from then on.
|`corr` |Prediction corrections per second, and the size of the last one in pixels.
|===

A steady `rtt`, low `jitter` and `loss`, and a `corr` near 0 when nobody is near you make a good link. On an
original XPilot server every field is `-`.

[[scoring]]
== Scoring

Scoring follows XPilot's rules (the server's `Scoring` is a port of XPilot's `score.c`):

* Killing a player moves points from the victim to you. Between equal scores this is 10 points; you
  get more for killing a better player and fewer for killing a weaker one.
* Each way of killing has a multiplier: `shotKillScoreMult`, `laserKillScoreMult`,
  `runoverKillScoreMult`, and so on. Crashing into a wall costs points (`crashScoreMult`), as does
  killing yourself.
* In team play, scores can be shared within a team (`teamShareScore`); killing a team mate earns
  nothing. When a player leaves, their share is taken back and shared among the rest of the team.
* When a player leaves the game, their shots in flight disappear, unless the server sets `keepShots`.
* A paused player slowly loses points (0.01 per frame).

[[rounds]]
== Rounds, lives and game over

Without limits, the game never ends: you die, restart at your base and play on. The server options
below turn it into rounds. They are ported from XPilot and run in the server simulation today.

Limited lives:: With `limitedLives` on, each player has `worldLives` lives per round. Each death costs
one; after the last you are out of the round (marked `D` in the scoreboard) until it ends.
End of a round:: Without teams, the round ends when only one of several players is still alive (the
winner), when nobody is (a draw), or when the round time `maxRoundTime` (seconds) runs out (a draw).
In team play, a team is out when all its members are; the last team alive wins "by staying alive", and
when the time runs out the team that saved more treasures, then the team with the higher total score,
wins.
Bonuses:: At the end of a round the player with the best kills/(deaths+1) ratio gets a bonus as the
"Deadliest Player" (several tied players share it), and the winner, or the winning team's members, get
a winner's bonus.
Next round:: Everyone gets their lives back. With `reset` on (the default), every ship restarts at its base and all
shots and objects are cleared. The next round starts after `roundDelay` seconds ("Delaying N seconds
until start of next round."). With `roundsToPlay` set, the server announces "Round N out of M" and
after the last round declares "Game over..." with the best and worst players (and teams).
Joining during a round:: With limited lives, if a player (outside team play) has already lost a life
in this round, a newcomer waits for the next round, marked `W` in the scoreboard. With `resetOnHuman`
set to N, while at most N human players are in the game a newcomer ends the current round so that they
can play at once.

All of these options are in <<05-server-options.adoc#opts-race,Races, rounds and game length>> and
<<05-server-options.adoc#opts-ships,Ships and players>>.

Game length:: With `gameDuration` (minutes, short `time`), the whole game has a wall-clock limit that
starts when the first human joins. The HUD shows the time left. When it runs out the game is over
(`Game over...`), and 30 seconds later the server shuts down: a countdown shows on the HUD and every
player is disconnected.

[[commands]]
== Commands: `/team`, `/ally` and more

A chat line that starts with `/` is a command for the server, as in XPilot. Commands can be shortened
to any start of their name (`/t 2` is `/team 2`); the first command in alphabetical order that matches
wins, so `/l` (and even `/list`) is `/lock`. The reply comes back as a message marked
`[*Server reply*]`.

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Command |What it does
|`/help`, `/help CMD` |List the commands, or explain one.
|`/team N` |Move to team N (team play only).
|`/ally invite NAME`, `cancel`, `refuse [NAME]`, `accept [NAME]`, `leave`, `list` |Form and manage
alliances (<<alliances>>).
|`/get OPTION` |Show a server option's value, for example `/get gravity`.
|`/version` |Show the server's version.
|`/queue` |Show the players waiting to get in (<<04-running-a-server.adoc#joining,Joining>>).
|`/lock` |Show whether the game is locked against newcomers.
|`/password PW` |Become an operator (<<04-running-a-server.adoc#operators,Operator commands>>).
|===

[[alliances]]
=== Alliances

Outside team play (and if the server allows it, `allowAlliances`, on by default), players can form
alliances: temporary teams. Allies cannot hurt each other with `teamImmunity`, share points with
`teamShareScore`, lose points for killing each other, show as friends (blue) on the radar and HUD, are
skipped by "lock on closest" and are spared by each other's ECM.

* `/ally invite NAME` invites a player; `/ally accept NAME` (or just `/ally accept`) joins a player who
  invited you, `/ally refuse` declines, `/ally cancel` withdraws your invitation.
* `/ally leave` leaves your alliance; `/ally list` shows its members.
* Robots decide on invitations themselves.
* With `announceAlliances`, everyone is told about alliance changes and the score list shows each
  player's alliance number; otherwise only those involved are told, and your allies show as `+` in
  your score list.

[[ship-shapes]]
=== Ship shapes

You can fly a ship of your own design, if the server allows it (`allowShipShapes`); otherwise, and for
an invalid shape, you get the server's `defaultShipShape`. Give the shape with `--shape` (or
`--shipShape`) in XPilot's shape format, for example
`--shape "(NM:Arrow)(SH: 15,0 -8,8 -4,0 -8,-8)(MG: 15,0)(EN: -4,0)"`, or as a name: with a shape file
(`--shipShapeFile FILE`, one shape per line, each with its `(NM:name)`), `--shape Arrow` picks the shape
of that name. The same works from your keys file with `xpilot.shipShape:` and `xpilot.shipShapeFile:`,
as with the XPilot client. XPilot's size rules apply: points within 15 of the centre, and a ship at
least 8 pixels out in each direction. The shape is sent when you join; it cannot change during a game.

[[ball-games]]
== Ball games: treasures and balls

On maps with treasures (map symbol `*`) every team has a treasure with a ball resting in it. The goal is
to steal another team's ball and bring it to your own treasure.

* Fly close to a ball and hold the connector key (kbd:[Ctrl] or kbd:[Alt]/kbd:[Option]): a dashed
  connector reaches out. Pull away; when the line gets long enough (`ballConnectorLength`) it snaps
  solid and the ball is yours, on a springy tether. You can tow it, swing it and throw it; kbd:[D]
  drops it.
* Bring an enemy ball to your own treasure to destroy their treasure. Your team scores and the losing
  team loses points; with `treasureKillTeam` its players die too. The ball's home treasure gets a new
  ball only if it is not destroyed.
* Bring your own team's ball back to your treasure to replace it (5 points).
* With `captureTheFlag` (short `ctf`) you can only cash an enemy ball while your own ball is home: "Your
  treasure must be safe before you can cash an opponent's!".
* A loose ball hit by a ship, an asteroid or a shot dies and comes back home
  (`treasureCollisionDestroys`); with `treasureCollisionMayKill` a ball can kill an unshielded ship.

Empty treasures (`^`) have no ball of their own but count as a home for cashing. With
`treasuresOnRadar`, balls show on the radar.

[[targets]]
== Targets

Targets (map symbol `!`) belong to the team with the closest base. Shots damage enemy targets, a mine
destroys one at once, and a ship ramming one damages it; the target's crossed lines show the damage. A
destroyed target scores for the attacker's team and comes back after `targetDeadTime` seconds (with
`targetSync`, a team's targets all come back together); with `targetKillTeam`, destroying a team's last
target kills its players. Damaged targets slowly repair themselves; you can speed this up for your own
team's targets by holding the repair key (kbd:[F]) within about 2.5 blocks. With `targetTeamCollision`
off, your own team flies and shoots through its targets.

As in XPilot, the "target destroyed" sound is played at the map's origin rather than at the target, so
you only hear it near the bottom-left corner of the map.

[[races]]
== Races

On race maps (`timing`, short `race`) you fly through the checkpoints (`A`, `B`, `C`, ...) in order;
the next one is drawn filled and shown on the radar. Pass within `checkpointRadius` blocks of it.
Passing `A` starts a lap; after `raceLaps` laps you finish. The messages give your lap times and your
best lap ("NAME completes lap 2 in 21.36 s. Personal race best lap time: 20.71 s.").

Finishers score by position: the first gets the most, shared when several finish in the same frame. The
race ends when someone has finished (with limited lives, when at most one racer is left), or when
`maxRoundTime` runs out. The best lap of the race scores extra, and the next race starts with the bases
reassigned by best lap time. With `ballrace`, a ball carried through the checkpoints counts instead of
the ship (with `ballraceConnected`, only a ball on its tether).

In race mode the score list is ordered by race progress, the leader first, and a column shows each
racer's lap and checkpoint as XPilot shows them (for example `2.c`).

[[asteroids]]
== Asteroids

On maps with asteroids (`maxAsteroidDensity` above 0, more around the `&` concentrators) large rocks
drift and spin through the world. Shots and weapons break an asteroid into two smaller ones; the
smallest break into wreckage, and sometimes items (`asteroidItemProb`). Breaking one scores
`asteroidPoints`, up to `asteroidMaxScore`. Hitting an asteroid costs fuel unless your shield is fully
up, and with `asteroidCollisionMayKill` (on by default) it kills an unshielded ship. With `asteroidsOnRadar`
they show on the radar.

[[pausing]]
== Pausing

* kbd:[Pause] pauses you only when you are on your own home base and nearly stopped (slower than
  3 plus the local gravity). Anywhere else the key does nothing.
* A paused ship is taken out of play and marked `P` in the scoreboard. You lose 0.01 points per frame
  while paused.
* You can unpause only after a 10-second countdown. Press kbd:[Pause] again; you restart at your home
  base. With limited lives, outside team play, if you had already lost a life this round you wait for
  the next round instead (`W`).
* If you press no keys for 60 seconds while another human is playing, you are paused automatically.
* A player paused for longer than `maxPauseTime` seconds (default 3600; 0 disables it) is removed from
  the server.

All multipliers are in <<05-server-options.adoc#opts-scoring,Scoring options>>.

[[items]]
== Items and weapons

Items work on the JXPilot server and on original XPilot servers alike; the rules are XPilot's.

Items float around the map (more around item concentrators, the `%` map symbol); fly over one to pick
it up. On the map an item is a blue triangle with the item's symbol inside it, as in the "In play"
column; the HUD shows the symbols of the items you carry, with their counts. These are XPilot's 21
items. The pictures are drawn from the same bitmaps the client uses (ported from the XPilot client),
and the descriptions are the XPilot client's own.

ifdef::env-github[]
On GitHub, see link:generated/items.adoc[generated/items.adoc] for the table with the pictures.
endif::[]

include::generated/items.adoc[]

How often each item appears, how many you start with and how many you may carry are server options
(<<05-server-options.adoc#opts-items,Items and fuel>>). The keys below are JXPilot's defaults
(<<all-keys,All keys>>); the descriptions follow the XPilot manual.

[[dropping-items]]
=== Dropping items

kbd:[Keypad 0] (or keypad kbd:[Insert]) selects, press by press, which item to drop; keypad
kbd:[Delete] (or keypad kbd:[.]) drops it. A box on the HUD marks the selected item. Depending on the server (`loseItemDestroys`) the item is
destroyed or falls back into the map. Tanks cannot be dropped this way; they are detached
(<<tanks>>). When you are killed you may drop some of your items (`dropItemOnKillProb`).

[[weapons]]
=== Weapons

Shots:: kbd:[Enter] fires your cannon. *Wide-angle shot* items add two extra front cannons, *rear
shot* items an extra rear cannon.
Missiles:: A *rocket pack* can be fired three ways. kbd:[ \ ] fires a *smart missile*, which homes on
the player you have locked on (it needs a lock). kbd:[;] fires a *heat seeker*: faster, it homes on
engine heat, and ECM cannot confuse it; a detached tank fools it. kbd:[ ' ] fires a *torpedo*, very fast
and without guidance, straight ahead.
Mines:: kbd:[Tab] drops a stationary *mine*, kbd:[ \] ] throws a moving one (a bomb). Mines explode when
an enemy comes close; they spare you and your team. Your own and your team's mines look hollow, others
filled; an ECM can reprogram a mine, so a hollow mine is not always safe. kbd:[=] detonates the
closest mine you dropped.
Laser:: kbd:[/] fires a short-range laser beam in the direction you point: instant and deadly, but
expensive in fuel. More laser items give more range and a faster repeat. Enemy ECMs can destroy your
lasers.
Nuclear and cluster weapons:: If the server allows them (`allowNukes`, `allowClusters`; the bundled
`lib/defaults` turns both off), kbd:[N] makes your next missiles or mines nuclear: `N` takes eight
missiles or four mines for one weapon, `FN` (press again) uses your whole stock. kbd:[C] makes them
cluster weapons, which burst into a shower of deadly shots instead of debris: careful, the shots can
kill you too.

[[modifiers]]
=== Weapon modifiers

When the server allows modifiers (`allowModifiers`, on in `lib/defaults`), these keys change how your
missiles, mines and lasers behave. The current modifiers show at the bottom left of the HUD.

[cols="1,1,5",options="header"]
|===
|Key |HUD |Effect
|kbd:[N] |`N`, `FN` |Nuclear: limited, then full (needs `allowNukes`)
|kbd:[C] |`C` |Cluster (needs `allowClusters`)
|kbd:[I] |`I` |Implosion: explosions pull instead of push
|kbd:[V] |`V1`-`V3` |Slower explosion fragments (for nuclear and cluster weapons)
|kbd:[X] |`X2`-`X4` |Fire that many miniature missiles or mines at once, sharing the damage and cost
|kbd:[Z] |`Z1`-`Z3` |Narrower spread of miniature weapons and of wide-angle and rear shots
|kbd:[B] |`B1`-`B3` |Weaker tractor beams; missiles with stronger engines but smaller warheads
|kbd:[L] |`LS`, `LB` |Laser that stuns or blinds instead of killing (needs `allowLaserModifiers`)
|kbd:[K] | |Clear all modifiers
|kbd:[1]-kbd:[4] | |Load modifier bank 1 to 4
|===

Fill the banks in your keys file, in the XPilot client's format (`xpilot.modifierBank1: FN C V2 X4`,
up to `modifierBank4`); the client sends them to the server when you join. In the game, store your
current modifiers in a bank by pressing the backtick key `+`+` (`keyReprogram`) and then the bank's key
kbd:[1]-kbd:[4].

[[locks]]
=== Locking on

Smart missiles, tractor beams and the ECM's missile retargeting need a lock on a player. The locked
player is drawn filled and blinks on the radar, and the HUD shows their name, distance and direction
(<<gallery.adoc#on-screen-hud,The HUD>>).

[cols="2,4",options="header"]
|===
|Key |Locks on
|kbd:[Page Down] (or kbd:[→]) |the next player
|kbd:[Page Up] (or kbd:[←]) |the previous player
|kbd:[↑] (or kbd:[Select]) |the closest player, if within range
|kbd:[↓] |the next closest player
|kbd:[5]-kbd:[8] |the player stored in lock bank 1 to 4 (store one with the backtick key `+`+`, then the bank key)
|===

With `lockOtherTeam` off, you can only lock on your own team. With `allowViewing`, a paused or
waiting player sees the game through the locked player's eyes.

[[tanks]]
=== Tanks

Each *tank* item adds a fuel tank: more fuel capacity and quicker refuelling, but more weight.
kbd:[E] and kbd:[W] choose the active tank (the one fuel is used from); kbd:[R] detaches the active
tank (or the last extra tank). A detached tank looks like your ship, carries your name, thrusts away and
draws heat seekers after it. It never passes race checkpoints.
